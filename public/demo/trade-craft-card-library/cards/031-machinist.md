    ## Rewritten card

    This card is best read as a piece of infrastructure, not as a quaint job title. Machinist was a practical specialist within the Industrial Age. Using lathes, mills, gauges, dies, and tolerances to turn metal into interchangeable parts.

The craft mattered because it stabilized another activity: food, shelter, mobility, trade, recordkeeping, power, repair, or symbolic continuity. Why it mattered: Factories depend on precision.

The deeper lesson is that ordinary tools often hide extraordinary dependencies. Odd edge: The tool makes tools that make tools.

Representative occurrence: Britain, continental Europe, North America, Japan, and later industrial centers worldwide.
