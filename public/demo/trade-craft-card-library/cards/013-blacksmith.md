    The important fact is not only what the worker made, but what became possible because the work was reliable. Blacksmith was a practical specialist within the Iron Age. Heating and hammering iron into blades, hinges, nails, plows, chains, locks, and repairs.

The craft mattered because it stabilized another activity: food, shelter, mobility, trade, recordkeeping, power, repair, or symbolic continuity. Why it mattered: Iron scales agriculture and war.

The deeper lesson is that ordinary tools often hide extraordinary dependencies. Odd edge: The village smith is also repair infrastructure.
