    The important fact is not only what the worker made, but what became possible because the work was reliable. Hide worker was a practical specialist within the Stone Age. Scraping, stretching, smoking, softening, and sewing animal hide into clothing, tents, bags, drums, shields, and containers.

The craft mattered because it stabilized another activity: food, shelter, mobility, trade, recordkeeping, power, repair, or symbolic continuity. Why it mattered: Made climates habitable.

The deeper lesson is that ordinary tools often hide extraordinary dependencies. Odd edge: Brains, smoke, urine, and fat all become tools.
