    ## Rewritten card

    Embalmer sits where technique and legitimacy touch. Preparing bodies with salts, resins, wrappings, and ritual knowledge placed anatomical handling inside a sacred logistics system.

In the Bronze Age, work like this cannot be separated cleanly from belief, law, memory, rank, or public trust. The craft mattered because it produced an outcome other people were willing to recognize as valid. Why it mattered: Death became a long-duration material project.

The strange edge is this: Methods and status varied across periods and communities. The material act was only half the job. The other half was making the act socially legible.
