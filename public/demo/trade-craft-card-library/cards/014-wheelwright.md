    ## Rewritten card

    At first glance this is a small craft. In practice, it sits at a pressure point in the society around it. Wheelwright was a practical specialist within the Iron Age. Building wheels with hubs, spokes, rims, metal tires, and axle alignment. Precision mattered: bad wheels killed cargo and animals.

The craft mattered because it stabilized another activity: food, shelter, mobility, trade, recordkeeping, power, repair, or symbolic continuity. Why it mattered: Road economies require wheels.

The deeper lesson is that ordinary tools often hide extraordinary dependencies. Odd edge: Wood movement and metal shrinkage had to cooperate.
