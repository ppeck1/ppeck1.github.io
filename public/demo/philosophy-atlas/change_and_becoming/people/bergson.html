Core problems and positions

Answers: Is reality fundamentally process?

Reality as Pure Duration

Real time is lived duration — a continuous, indivisible flow of qualitative change — not the spatialized series of instants used by science. Intellect freezes reality into static snapshots; intuition grasps the ceaseless becoming that is the real.

Historical setting

Bergson’s change philosophy emerges in a period shaped by evolutionary theory, experimental psychology, mechanistic explanation, and mathematical physics. His complaint is not that science is useless, but that intellect tends to spatialize time: it cuts living duration into inert units and then mistakes the cut-up representation for lived becoming.

Duration versus spatialized time

For Bergson, real time is not a container filled with successive points. It is interpenetrating qualitative continuity: memory thickens the present, and life moves as creative advance. This makes change internally continuous rather than a sequence of static snapshots.

Cautions and uses

Bergson criticizes the metaphysical overreach of spatialized intellect, not disciplined inquiry as such. His account remains useful wherever measurement and lived continuity diverge: consciousness, memory, evolution, art, and systems whose temporal texture is damaged by over-discretization.

Historical setting

Henri Bergson wrote at the turn of the twentieth century amid debates about evolution, psychology, time, and scientific description. His lectures and books reached a broad public.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

Bergson distinguishes lived duration from time treated as a sequence of spatialized units. The distinction tests whether measurement has silently replaced the phenomenon measured.
