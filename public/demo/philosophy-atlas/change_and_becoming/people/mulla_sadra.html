Substantial Motion

Against the Aristotelian view that only accidents change while substance stays fixed, Mulla Sadra holds that substance itself is in continuous renewal. The world is a single graded flow of being constantly intensifying toward its source.

Historical setting

Mulla Sadra enters the change problem after centuries of debate about Avicennian essence and existence, Suhrawardi’s illuminationism, kalam theology, and mystical accounts of being. His theory of substantial motion is not merely a local physics claim. It is part of a broader metaphysics in which existence is primary, graded, and dynamically intensified.

Substantial motion

Aristotelian frameworks often locate change in qualities, places, quantities, or accidental states while substance persists. Mulla Sadra radicalizes this by arguing that corporeal substance itself is in motion. The world is continuously renewed; identity is a trajectory of existential intensification rather than a static bearer underneath change.

Cautions and transmission

Mulla Sadra is historically connected to Islamic philosophical and theological traditions, not directly to Whiteheadian process thought. Comparisons with process metaphysics are useful, but they should be marked as later comparison unless a specific reception pathway is documented.

Historical setting

Mullā Ṣadrā worked in Safavid Iran within Islamic philosophical, theological, mystical, and commentarial traditions. His system develops through engagement with prior Peripatetic, Illuminationist, and religious sources.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

Substantial motion rethinks the relation between identity and change: transformation can reach substance itself rather than merely its accidental properties.

Texts and transmission

The al-Asfār and related works survive in manuscript and print traditions with extensive later teaching and commentary. Translation remains partial and terminology-sensitive.
