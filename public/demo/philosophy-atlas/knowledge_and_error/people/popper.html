Answers: How is error detected and corrected?

Knowledge Grows by Falsification

We can never verify a universal theory, but a single counter-instance can refute it. Science advances not by proving theories true but by boldly conjecturing and ruthlessly attempting to falsify them. Knowledge is irreducibly fallible and grows through error-elimination.

FalsificationFallibilism

Strict falsificationism faces the Duhem-Quine problem: theories are tested in bundles, so refutation is rarely clean.

Historical setting

Popper’s problem is how science learns from error. He rejects the idea that scientific theories are proven by accumulating confirmations. Theories gain scientific status by taking risks: they forbid possible observations and can therefore be tested severely.

Conjecture and refutation

Popper’s loop is evolutionary: propose, test, expose error, revise. Falsifiability is a demarcation criterion, not a guarantee that a theory is false or worthless. The focus is institutional and methodological: build practices that make error visible rather than protected.

Cautions

Actual scientific testing involves auxiliaries, measurement assumptions, background theory, and judgment. Popper remains useful as a norm of vulnerability to correction, but simplistic falsificationism can misdescribe scientific practice.

Historical setting

Karl Popper worked in twentieth-century philosophy of science and political thought against the background of European crisis, exile, and debates about scientific rationality.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

Falsifiability is a demarcation proposal and methodological pressure: theories should expose themselves to possible failure rather than merely accumulate confirming instances.

Texts and transmission

The Logic of Scientific Discovery, Conjectures and Refutations, and later revisions should be distinguished. Scientific practice is richer than any single slogan.
