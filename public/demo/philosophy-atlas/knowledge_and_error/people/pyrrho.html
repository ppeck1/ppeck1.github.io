Answers: Is certainty possible?

Suspension of Judgment for Tranquility

Since for every argument an equal counter-argument can be found, we should suspend judgment on all non-evident matters. This suspension, far from being paralysing, yields ataraxia — freedom from the disturbance of dogmatic commitment.

Historical setting

Pyrrho himself left no writings. His profile is mediated through later reports, especially Timon and later skeptical traditions. The responsible atlas stance is cautious: Pyrrho anchors a lineage of suspension and tranquility, but details of his exact doctrines are difficult to recover.

Suspension and tranquility

Pyrrhonian skepticism is not simple disbelief. It uses equipollence, or balanced opposition among appearances and arguments, to suspend dogmatic commitment. In later reception, suspension is connected with ataraxia, tranquility, though the exact causal relation is debated.

Cautions

The Pyrrhonian does not necessarily claim that knowledge is impossible, since that would itself be a dogmatic claim. The practice is more subtle: continue with appearances and customs while withholding assent from non-evident theoretical claims.

Historical setting

Pyrrho of Elis is known largely through later reports rather than surviving writings. His historical reconstruction depends on mediated testimony and later skeptical traditions.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

The pressure is practical and epistemic: when opposing appearances and arguments remain unresolved, suspension of judgment may reduce dogmatic disturbance.

Texts and transmission

Because Pyrrho left no texts, later witnesses and the distinction between Pyrrho and Pyrrhonism require explicit separation.
