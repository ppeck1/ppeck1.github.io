Answers: What is knowledge?

Justified True Belief Is Not Knowledge

Gettier presented cases where a belief is true and justified yet intuitively not knowledge, because its truth is a matter of luck disconnected from the justification. This broke the classical analysis and launched decades of attempts to add a fourth condition.

The Gettier problem is a challenge, not a positive theory; no consensus repair has emerged.

Historical setting

The 1963 Gettier paper is brief but decisive because it shows that justified true belief can still be true by luck. It did not create the knowledge problem, but it forced a major reorganization of twentieth-century epistemology around luck, safety, sensitivity, reliability, virtue, and defeaters.

JTB failure pattern

Gettier-style cases separate justification from the right kind of connection to truth. They expose a structural gap: justification can support a belief that happens to be true through a deviant path. This makes error not only false belief but misconnected truth.

Cautions

They show that a simple tripartite analysis is incomplete. Later theories attempt to add anti-luck conditions, reliability, causal connection, virtue, safety, sensitivity, or defeater constraints. The atlas should map Gettier as a hinge, not an endpoint.

Knowledge Through Examined Ignorance

Gettier targets the justified-true-belief analysis often traced to Plato's Socrates, showing justification plus truth can still fall short of knowledge.

opposition · interpretive

Knowledge as Valid Cognition (Pramana)

Dignaga grounds knowledge in the reliable operation of cognitive instruments (perception, inference); Gettier shows that on a justification-based analysis, reliable-seeming justification can still misfire — two different diagnoses of when cognition counts as knowing.
