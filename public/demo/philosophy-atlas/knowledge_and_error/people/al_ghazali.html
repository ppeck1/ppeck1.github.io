Answers: Is certainty possible?

From Radical Doubt to Certainty

Al-Ghazali subjected the senses and reason to systematic doubt, finding even reason shakeable. He emerged from the crisis not through a further argument but through a light cast into the heart by God, grounding certainty in immediate, non-discursive insight.

SkepticismThe Indubitable

His resolution is theological and experiential; reading it as a purely epistemic argument like Descartes' misses its Sufi grounding.

Historical setting

Al-Ghazali’s epistemology is not reducible to anti-philosophy. He mastered the philosophical tradition he criticized and staged a crisis over sense perception, reason, authority, and inner illumination. The Deliverance from Error presents knowledge as a lived problem, not merely a formal criterion.

Doubt, causality, and illumination

Al-Ghazali questions whether sense and reason can secure themselves absolutely. His critique of necessary causality in the philosophers also opens space for theological occasionalism. Yet he does not reject all reasoning; he limits its authority and reorders it within a theological and spiritual path.

Cautions

That story is historically crude. Later Islamic philosophy continued in multiple forms. Al-Ghazali should be represented as a critic, theologian, jurist, Sufi, and epistemic diagnostician whose reception is complex.

Historical setting

Abū Ḥāmid al-Ghazālī wrote in the eleventh-century Islamic world across law, theology, spirituality, education, and philosophical critique. His work belongs inside that intellectual ecology, not only in a European causality story.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

His critiques test the authority claimed for demonstrations. In causality debates, the issue is not a casual denial of regularity but the warrant for necessary connection and divine agency.

Texts and transmission

Deliverance from Error, The Incoherence of the Philosophers, and the wider corpus have distinct purposes. Arabic texts, later commentaries, and translations require differentiation.
