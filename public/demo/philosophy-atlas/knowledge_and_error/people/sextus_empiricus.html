Core problems and positions

Answers: Is certainty possible?

The Skeptical Modes

Sextus systematized skepticism into 'modes' — recurring patterns of disagreement and relativity that show why no claim about the non-evident can compel assent. Skepticism is presented as an ongoing practice of inquiry, not a dogma that knowledge is impossible.

SkepticismSuspension of Judgment (Epoche)

Pyrrhonian skepticism is a method aimed at tranquility, not the self-refuting claim 'nothing can be known' often attributed to it.

Historical setting

Sextus writes in a mature philosophical environment where dogmatic schools have elaborate theories of criterion, cause, sign, logic, physics, and ethics. His works are invaluable not only for skepticism but also for preserving arguments against rival schools.

Skeptical method

The skeptical method is procedural. It does not need to refute every claim permanently; it sets arguments in opposition until dogmatic compulsion weakens. The skeptic lives according to appearances, natural feelings, laws, customs, and arts without adding metaphysical certainty.

Cautions

The method can look destructive, but its internal aim is freedom from rash assent and disturbance. In knowledge-and-error terms, Sextus is a disciplined error-control system: slow assent where criteria exceed available warrant.

Historical setting

Sextus Empiricus is a principal surviving source for ancient Pyrrhonian skepticism, likely writing around the second or third century CE in a medical and philosophical environment.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

His method organizes countervailing considerations so that assent is suspended when claims outrun warrant. The point is practice, not a new dogma that nothing can be known.

Texts and transmission

Outlines of Pyrrhonism and Against the Mathematicians survive through manuscript and print transmission. Terminology and work boundaries matter.
