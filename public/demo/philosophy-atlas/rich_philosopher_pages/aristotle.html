Identity and orientation

Aristotle worked across the intellectual world of fourth-century BCE Greece and Macedon. His corpus spans logic, natural inquiry, metaphysics, ethics, politics, rhetoric, poetics, and biological investigation.

Epistemic caution
Roman and Renaissance busts are reception images, not verified contemporary likenesses. Aristotle’s authority should neither be dismissed wholesale nor treated as timeless evidence.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

He repeatedly resists one-factor explanations. Potentiality and actuality answer the problem of change; the four causes distinguish different explanatory questions; biological observation disciplines abstraction.

What persists through change?

position_answers_question

reinterpretation · interpretive

Being Is, and Cannot Change

Aristotle accepts Parmenides cannot have being from non-being, but reframes the problem with potentiality so change need not violate it.

critique · interpretive

Everything Flows (Panta Rhei)

Aristotle preserves change but rejects total flux: a persisting substrate is required for change to be intelligible.

opposition · interpretive

Emptiness Makes Change Possible

Both make change intelligible, but oppositely: Aristotle needs a persisting essence-bearing substrate; Nagarjuna holds that the absence of inherent essence is what permits change.

opposition · interpretive

Substantial Motion

Sadra directly overturns the Aristotelian premise that substance is fixed while only accidents change.
