Al-Ghazali

Persian theologian and Sufi who passed through radical doubt of senses and reason to a certainty grounded in divine illumination.

Identity and orientation

Abū Ḥāmid al-Ghazālī wrote in the eleventh-century Islamic world across law, theology, spirituality, education, and philosophical critique. His work belongs inside that intellectual ecology, not only in a European causality story.

Epistemic caution
Traditional images are not verified portraits. Do not reduce al-Ghazālī to “occasionalism” as if one label exhausted the corpus.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

His critiques test the authority claimed for demonstrations. In causality debates, the issue is not a casual denial of regularity but the warrant for necessary connection and divine agency.
