Heraclitus

Greek thinker of Ephesus who held that all things flow and that the unity of reality lies in the strife of opposites governed by logos.

Identity and orientation

Heraclitus is associated with Ephesus around 500 BCE. The city’s civic, commercial, imperial, and religious pressures form a plausible background for a philosophy of measured transformation.

Epistemic caution
Biographical anecdotes are late. “Everything flows” is a useful compression but should not replace the surviving fragment record.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

His fragments repeatedly place tension, opposition, and logos together. Order is not the absence of conflict; it is a pattern legible through transformation.

Is change real, or an illusion?

position_answers_question

opposition · interpretive

Being Is, and Cannot Change

The founding opposition of Western metaphysics: total flux versus changeless being. The entire subsequent debate is a response to this clash.

critique · interpretive

Change as Actualization of Potential

Aristotle preserves change but rejects total flux: a persisting substrate is required for change to be intelligible.

comparative_parallel_without_known_contact · comparative_no_contact_assumed

The Transformation of Things

Greek flux and Daoist transformation both make ceaseless change fundamental, though Zhuangzi draws an ethic of yielding rather than a logos of strife.

development · inferred

Becoming as Dialectical Development

Hegel praised Heraclitus, claiming there was no proposition of Heraclitus he had not taken up into his Logic; dialectical becoming systematizes flux.
