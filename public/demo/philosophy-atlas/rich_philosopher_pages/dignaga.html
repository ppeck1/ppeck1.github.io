Dignaga

Buddhist logician who founded a rigorous epistemology built on perception and inference as the means of valid cognition.

Identity and orientation

Dignāga is central to Buddhist epistemology in the Indian scholastic world of the fifth and sixth centuries CE. His work reorganizes debate about valid cognition, perception, inference, and language.

Epistemic caution
Traditional iconography is not portrait evidence. Do not collapse pramāṇa theory into a simple empiricism analogue.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

The analysis separates kinds of warrant: perception and inference do different work. Conceptual construction and exclusion become central to explaining linguistic cognition.

Justified True Belief Is Not Knowledge

Dignaga grounds knowledge in the reliable operation of cognitive instruments (perception, inference); Gettier shows that on a justification-based analysis, reliable-seeming justification can still misfire — two different diagnoses of when cognition counts as knowing.
