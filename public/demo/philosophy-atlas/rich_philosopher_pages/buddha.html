Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha)

Founder of Buddhism. His analysis of the person into impermanent aggregates and his doctrine of no-self (anatta) reframed the self as a process to be seen through rather than a substance to be defended.

Identity and orientation

The historical Buddha is placed in the north Indian śramaṇa environment of the first millennium BCE. Traditional biographies and critical historical reconstruction overlap but are not identical.

Epistemic caution
Early Buddhist art includes aniconic and later iconic forms. Do not treat any image as a contemporary portrait or flatten dependent origination into a single linear diagram.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

The teaching responds to suffering through a disciplined analysis of causes, attachment, impermanence, and practice. No-self is embedded in a therapeutic and ethical path rather than offered as an isolated metaphysical slogan.

Bundle Theory

The view that an object — including a self — is nothing over and above a collection of properties or perceptions.

Self as a Bundle of Perceptions

opposition · interpretive

Self as Witness Consciousness

Advaita affirms an unchanging witness-self; Buddhism denies any permanent self. A core preserved disagreement, not to be reconciled.
