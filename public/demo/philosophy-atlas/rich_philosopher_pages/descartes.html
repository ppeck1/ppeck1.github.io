Rene Descartes

French philosopher who made the self-certainty of the thinking subject the foundation of knowledge, defining the self as an immaterial thinking substance.

Identity and orientation

René Descartes wrote amid early modern scientific change, confessional conflict, and disputes about inherited scholastic authority. His method seeks a secure starting point for knowledge while preserving a mechanistic natural philosophy.

Epistemic caution
Do not compress the project into “mind versus body” alone. Portraits may be historical artworks, not photographs; edition records require provenance.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

Methodic doubt is strategic rather than a permanent celebration of uncertainty. The cogito establishes a point of certainty from which Descartes attempts to rebuild knowledge.

From Radical Doubt to Certainty

Both pass through radical, self-imposed doubt of senses and reason to reach certainty — a Persian Sufi five centuries before a French rationalist — though Ghazali's resolution is illumination, Descartes' is argument.

opposition · interpretive

The Skeptical Modes

Descartes deploys skeptical doubt only to defeat it with an indubitable foundation; the Pyrrhonist holds that no such foundation compels assent and judgment should stay suspended.

opposition · interpretive

Belief as Custom, Not Reason

Where Descartes seeks rational certainty as the ground of knowledge, Hume denies reason can justify our core factual beliefs at all, grounding them in custom.

opposition · interpretive

Knowledge Grows by Falsification

Foundationalist certainty versus fallibilist error-elimination: Descartes seeks an indubitable base; Popper denies any is needed or available and makes refutation the engine of knowledge.
