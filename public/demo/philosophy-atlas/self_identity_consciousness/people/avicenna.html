Core problems and positions

Answers: What is the self?

The Floating Man / Self-Awareness

In the 'floating man' thought experiment, a person created in midair with no sensory input would still affirm their own existence. Avicenna concludes the self is immediately self-aware and is an immaterial substance, knowable independently of the body.

SubstanceConsciousness

Often cited as anticipating Descartes' cogito, but Avicenna's metaphysical aims differ; the parallel is suggestive, not identity.

Historical setting

Avicenna inherits Aristotelian psychology but transforms it through an Islamic philosophical setting. The soul is not merely a biological form; it has immaterial self-awareness and a complex relation to intellect, body, and afterlife debates.

Flying man and self-awareness

Avicenna imagines a person created fully formed but suspended, with no sensory contact. Such a person would, he argues, affirm his own existence even without awareness of body parts. The point is not Cartesian doubt, but the immediacy of self-awareness and the soul’s non-reducibility to bodily perception.

Cautions

There are meaningful comparisons with the cogito, but the textual setting differs. Avicenna’s argument sits within a theory of soul, intellect, emanation, medicine, and Islamic philosophical theology. Label any Descartes connection as comparison unless citing a transmission pathway.

Historical setting

Ibn Sīnā worked in the Persianate Islamic intellectual world of the eleventh century across courts, libraries, medicine, and philosophy. His synthesis is not merely a bridge to Europe; it is a major intellectual system in its own context.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

The flying-man thought experiment isolates self-awareness from ordinary sensory dependence. His psychology, metaphysics, medicine, and logic are internally connected.

Texts and transmission

Arabic manuscript traditions, Persian context, later Islamic commentaries, Hebrew and Latin translations, and scholastic reception form multiple transmission paths.
