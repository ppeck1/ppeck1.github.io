Answers: What is the self?

Self as a Bundle of Perceptions

When I introspect, I never catch a 'self' — only particular perceptions. The self is nothing but a bundle of perceptions in perpetual flux, linked by resemblance and causation; its unity is a fiction of imagination.

Bundle TheoryNo-Self (Anatta)

Hume himself expressed dissatisfaction with his account in the Appendix; the bundle view is unstable on his own terms.

Historical setting

Hume’s self theory follows from his copy principle and empiricist psychology. If ideas derive from impressions, then a stable self-substance should be traceable to some impression. Hume reports finding only particular perceptions: heat, cold, love, pain, memory, desire, and so on.

Bundle theory

Hume does not find a simple, identical self behind experience. What appears as identity is constructed through associative relations among perceptions. Personal identity is therefore a product of imagination and habit, not discovery of a metaphysical owner.

Cautions

The resemblance to no-self is real enough for comparative work, but Hume’s method and aims differ. He is not offering a path of liberation; he is analyzing the limits of empiricist justification and the psychological mechanisms that make self-belief natural.

Historical setting

David Hume wrote within the Scottish Enlightenment and an empiricist debate about mind, causation, religion, and moral judgment. His project studies what experience can actually warrant.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

Hume’s pressure test is psychological and epistemic: impressions, ideas, custom, and habit explain much of what reason is often credited with doing. The analysis is skeptical without simply abandoning ordinary life.

Texts and transmission

The Treatise, Enquiries, Dialogues, essays, and posthumous publication history should be distinguished. His own revisions matter when presenting mature positions.

Visual archive notes

Do not reduce Hume to a slogan that causation is unreal. His target is the warrant for necessary connection and the role of custom. Visual references in the shared rich page remain external discovery lanes unless a file-level rights record is explicitly stored.

Used, transformed, and recoverable

Still strongly used: Problem of induction; Habit in causal expectation. Used, transformed: Bundle theory in self debates. Should be recovered: Difference between skeptical analysis and ordinary practice.

Common misuse and interpretive cautions

“Hume denied causation” oversimplification. Do not reduce Hume to a slogan that causation is unreal. His target is the warrant for necessary connection and the role of custom.
