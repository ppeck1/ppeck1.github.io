Answers: What is the self?

Self as Thinking Substance

Whatever I doubt, I cannot doubt that I think; therefore I exist as a thinking thing. The self is an immaterial thinking substance, distinct in nature from extended bodily substance.

SubstanceConsciousness

The cogito establishes thinking is occurring; the inference to a persisting thinking substance is precisely what Hume and Kant later contest.

Historical setting

Descartes’ self theory is tied to a program for rebuilding knowledge. The thinking self appears as what survives radical doubt: not first as a full personality, but as the indubitable activity of thinking. This occurs in a context of mathematical physics, mechanistic explanation, theological commitments, and critique of inherited scholastic forms.

Cogito and thinking substance

The cogito establishes that while doubting, thinking cannot be separated from the existence of the thinker. Descartes then argues for mind as res cogitans, distinct from extended body. In the atlas, the key is to distinguish the narrow certainty of the cogito from the broader metaphysical dualism built around it.

Cautions

“I think, therefore I am” is often detached from the Meditations’ disciplined method. Descartes is not simply saying that thought creates reality. He is using doubt to find an indubitable starting point for knowledge, then arguing into a larger metaphysical system.

Historical setting

René Descartes wrote amid early modern scientific change, confessional conflict, and disputes about inherited scholastic authority. His method seeks a secure starting point for knowledge while preserving a mechanistic natural philosophy.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

Methodic doubt is strategic rather than a permanent celebration of uncertainty. The cogito establishes a point of certainty from which Descartes attempts to rebuild knowledge.

Texts and transmission

The Discourse, Meditations, Principles, correspondence, and objections-and-replies form an interconnected record. Editions and translations should distinguish Descartes’s text from later textbook dualism.
