Core problems and positions

Answers: What is the self?

Soul as the Form of the Body

The soul is not a separate substance trapped in a body but the form, or first actuality, of a living body. It is the organizing principle of the organism's capacities. Most of the soul cannot exist apart from the body.

Soul as FormSubstance

Aristotle's hylomorphism resists both Cartesian dualism and modern physicalist reduction; mapping it onto either distorts it.

Why Aristotle thought the way he did

Aristotle’s system is not an abstract machine dropped from nowhere. It is a response to Plato, medicine, biology, Macedonian politics, civic instability, and the demand to explain changing things without reducing them to chaos.

Historical setting

Aristotle worked across the intellectual world of fourth-century BCE Greece and Macedon. His corpus spans logic, natural inquiry, metaphysics, ethics, politics, rhetoric, poetics, and biological investigation.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

He repeatedly resists one-factor explanations. Potentiality and actuality answer the problem of change; the four causes distinguish different explanatory questions; biological observation disciplines abstraction.

Texts and transmission

The surviving corpus is largely lecture-treatise material transmitted through ancient editing, manuscript copying, Arabic and Latin translation, commentarial traditions, and modern critical editions.

Visual archive notes

Roman and Renaissance busts are reception images, not verified contemporary likenesses. Aristotle’s authority should neither be dismissed wholesale nor treated as timeless evidence. Used, transformed, and recoverable

Still strongly used: Multi-causal explanation; Virtue and practical judgment; Biological observation. Used, transformed: Substance metaphysics; Teleology in biology. Should be recovered: Four-cause diagnostic as a systems prompt.

Common misuse and interpretive cautions
