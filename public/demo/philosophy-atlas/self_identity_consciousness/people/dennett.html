Answers: What is the self?

Self as Center of Narrative Gravity

The self is not a thing in the brain but an abstraction — a 'center of narrative gravity' the brain generates to organize behavior, like a fictional character's center of mass. Useful and real as a pattern, but not a substantial inner entity.

Stream of ConsciousnessConsciousness

Critics argue this explains the self away rather than explaining it; the relationship to the 'hard problem' is contested.

Historical setting

Dennett’s self theory belongs to a late twentieth-century context where computation, neuroscience, language, and evolution reshape philosophy of mind. He resists Cartesian theater models that imagine a central inner witness watching representations.

Narrative center of gravity

Dennett compares the self to a center of gravity: real as an explanatory abstraction, not as a separate substance. Narratives, habits, brain processes, social interpretation, and agency-modeling create a stable enough self-pattern without requiring an inner homunculus.

Cautions

Dennett is often read as saying consciousness or self simply does not exist. More carefully, he denies certain inflated inner-theater models and reinterprets selfhood as a pattern. Ethical and practical personhood are not erased by rejecting a Cartesian ego.

Historical setting

Daniel Dennett worked in late twentieth- and early twenty-first-century philosophy of mind alongside cognitive science, artificial intelligence, biology, and public debate.

Why this thinker reasoned this way

His multiple-drafts approach resists a single inner Cartesian theater. Explanatory pressure should move toward distributed processes rather than a hidden central witness.
