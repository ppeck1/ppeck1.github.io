> **TL;DR:** The Pre-Industrialization Knowledge Canon is a local set of standalone HTML atlases about the knowledge a community needs before modern industrial systems hide the work: food, water, shelter, repair, trust, teaching, memory, and meaning.

## What it is

This project explores pre-industrial knowledge as a systems map rather than nostalgia. The local artifact set includes essays and atlas pages on craft, growing food, people and fallacies, and a kid-readable knowledge canon.

The central frame is simple enough to teach and strong enough to organize:

- Provision: what keeps bodies alive
- Protection: what bounds harm and keeps trust repairable
- Meaning: what keeps memory, learning, and purpose coherent

That structure turns practical knowledge into a readable library. Farming, seed saving, tool repair, food preservation, warning systems, law, apprenticeship, stories, humility, and stewardship become connected rooms instead of isolated trivia.

## Why it matters

Modern systems often hide the dependencies that make daily life possible. This atlas pulls those dependencies back into view. It asks what knowledge decays when no one practices it, what a community must remember, and how to teach useful abstractions without losing the material ground underneath them.

The work also fits the broader portfolio pattern: use plain language, visible structure, and bounded claims to make complex systems easier to navigate.

## Current shape

The current local folder contains multiple standalone HTML artifacts, including:

- a craft-first pre-industrial lens
- a dense orthogonally-binary craft specification
- a growing-food expansion demo
- a people-and-fallacies expansion
- a kid-readable Pre-Industrial Knowledge Canon

Those pieces are not yet packaged as a public repo or production site. This page is the portfolio-safe summary layer.

## What This Demonstrates

- Educational knowledge design for systems literacy
- Plain-language translation of complex abstractions
- Durable standalone HTML artifacts
- A bridge between craft, resilience, cognition, and community-scale operations
