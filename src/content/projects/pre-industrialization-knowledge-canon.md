## What it is

HTML atlases exploring knowledge needed before modern systems hid the work. Organized around three frames:

- **Provision**: what keeps bodies alive (food, water, shelter, heat)
- **Protection**: what bounds harm and keeps trust repairable (warning systems, repair, law, bounded blame)
- **Meaning**: what sustains memory, learning, and purpose (teaching, stories, ritual, apprenticeship)

## What it is NOT

Not a reading list, nostalgia, or how-to manual.

## What it IS

A systems map of practical knowledge. Farming, seed saving, tool repair, food preservation, warning systems, law, apprenticeship, stories, humility, stewardship—these become connected rooms instead of isolated trivia.

## Why it matters

Modern systems hide dependencies that make daily life possible. This atlas pulls them back into view. Asks: What knowledge decays when no one practices it? What must a community remember? How do you teach useful abstractions without losing the material ground underneath?

## Current artifacts

## What This Demonstrates

Educational knowledge design for systems literacy without flattening material ground. How to make dependencies visible without prescribing solutions. Durable standalone HTML as knowledge infrastructure.
